# Parla Italiano! · Lesson 1.1 · The Italian Alphabet

## How to read this script

- **NARRATOR**: the only lines you record, in English. One file per line, named as shown (`n01.wav`, `n02.wav`...). Leave about half a second of silence at the start and end of each file.
- **IT (TTS)**: Italian spoken by a text-to-speech voice (Giulia = female voice, Orso = male voice). You don't record these.
- **SCREEN**: text and pictures that appear. Pronunciation guides are written for English speakers.
- Timings are targets. I'll fit the animation to your actual recordings.

**PICTURE:** A closed green book on a wooden desk. It snaps open; a Florence hotel desk pops up. Orso, suitcase in hand, waves at Giulia behind the desk.

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "Buongiorno! Come si chiama?"
**SCREEN (bubble):** *Buongiorno! Come si chiama?* → "Hello! What's your name?"

> **n01 · NARRATOR:** "Uh-oh. Orso just spelled his name in *English*… in Italy. Let's fix that: here's the Italian alphabet in under three minutes."

## 3. Only 21 letters (0:15–0:33)

**PICTURE:** Top-down on the open book: 26 English letter cards stand in a row. Five of them, **J K W X Y**, jump off the page and land in a paper box labelled **"FOREIGN WORDS"**: *jeans, ketchup, weekend, taxi, yogurt*. 21 cards remain.

> **n03 · NARRATOR:** "Good news: the Italian alphabet is shorter than yours. Just twenty-one letters. J, K, W, X and Y only show up in foreign words, like jeans, ketchup, or taxi."

**PICTURE:** Giulia taps the row; the 21 letters light up in green, white and red.

> **n04 · NARRATOR:** "And Italian is read the way it's written. Learn the letters, and you can already read."

## 4. The vowels: the big trap for English speakers (0:33–1:05)

**PICTURE:** Five large vowel cards pop up: **A E I O U**. Orso reads them the English way; each card wobbles and turns red: "ay, ee, eye, oh, you".

> **n05 · NARRATOR:** "Careful: this is where English speakers slip. Italian vowels are short and pure, and two of them swap places."

**PICTURE:** Giulia flips each card with a pull-tab. Each card shows the letter, its sound and an example word with a pop-up picture.

**IT (TTS, Giulia), one at a time:** "A… amore. E… Elena. I… Italia. O… oro. U… uva."

**SCREEN, one per card:**
- **A** [ah] → *amore* (love) ❤️
- **E** [eh] → *Elena* 👩
- **I** [ee] → *Italia* 🇮🇹
- **O** [oh] → *oro* (gold) 🪙
- **U** [oo] → *uva* (grapes) 🍇

> **n06 · NARRATOR:** "Italian E sounds like the E in 'bed'. And Italian I sounds like your E, 'ee'. So 'E' is 'eh'… and 'I' is 'ee'."

**PICTURE:** The E and I cards physically swap places with a paper "swish". Orso scratches his head, then gives a thumbs-up.

## 5. The letters with surprises (1:05–1:40)

**PICTURE:** Page turn. A pop-up Italian street: each shop sign is a letter. The camera tracks past them.

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "Acca." **SCREEN:** **H** = *acca* [AHK-kah] · silent in words: *hotel* = "otel"

> **n07 · NARRATOR:** "H is called 'acca', and it's silent. Hotel is just 'otel'."

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "Ci. Gi." **SCREEN:** **C** = *ci* [chee] · **G** = *gi* [jee]

> **n08 · NARRATOR:** "C is 'chee' and G is 'jee', like the start of 'cheese' and 'jeans'."

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "Cu." **SCREEN:** **Q** = *cu* [koo]

> **n09 · NARRATOR:** "Q is 'koo', never 'kyoo'."

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "Erre. Roma." **SCREEN:** **R** = *erre* [ER-reh] · rolled R 🌀

> **n10 · NARRATOR:** "R is 'erre', and you roll it with the tip of your tongue, like a little motor."

**PICTURE:** Orso tries; a paper motorbike sputters out of his mouth. Giulia laughs.

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "Zeta." **SCREEN:** **Z** = *zeta* [DZEH-tah]

> **n11 · NARRATOR:** "And Z is 'zeta', with a 'dz' sound."

**SCREEN (quick strip):** F *effe* · L *elle* · M *emme* · N *enne* · S *esse* · V *vu*

> **n12 · NARRATOR:** "Many letters end with an 'eh': effe, elle, emme, enne, esse. Easy!"

## 6. Spell like an Italian (1:40–2:10)

**PICTURE:** Back at the hotel desk. A pull-tab slides out a paper map of Italy; city names pop up as Giulia speaks.

> **n13 · NARRATOR:** "On the phone, Italians spell with city names. Not 'A as in apple', but 'A come Ancona': A like Ancona."

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "A come Ancona. B come Bologna. R come Roma. S come Savona."
**SCREEN:** *come* [KOH-meh] = "like / as" · A **Ancona** · B **Bologna** · R **Roma** · S **Savona**

> **n14 · NARRATOR:** "A double letter? Just say 'doppia', double: Rossi is erre, o, doppia esse, i."

**SCREEN:** *Rossi* → R · O · **doppia S** · I
