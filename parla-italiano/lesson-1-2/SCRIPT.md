## 3. Five pure vowels (0:14–0:45)

**PICTURE:** Five big vowel cards: **A E I O U**. Orso reads *vino* and *sole* the English way; the vowels stretch out into wobbly paper ribbons: "vee-no-oo", "so-leɪ".

> **n03 · NARRATOR:** "English speakers glide their vowels: 'oh-oo', 'ay-ee'. Italian vowels are short, clean and steady. No glide."

**PICTURE:** Giulia snips each ribbon with paper scissors; each card snaps back to a short, neat shape.

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "Casa. Bene. Vino. Sole. Uno."
**SCREEN, one per card:**
- **A** [ah] → *casa* [KAH-zah] house 🏠
- **E** [eh] → *bene* [BEH-neh] well 👍
- **I** [ee] → *vino* [VEE-noh] wine 🍷
- **O** [oh] → *sole* [SOH-leh] sun ☀️
- **U** [oo] → *uno* [OO-noh] one ☝️

> **n04 · NARRATOR:** "And every vowel is spoken, even at the end. 'Bene', not 'been'. 'Notte', not 'not'."

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "Notte."
**SCREEN:** *notte* [NOHT-teh] night 🌙: say the final **e**!

> **n05 · NARRATOR:** "E and O can be a little open or a little closed, depending on the region. Don't worry about it. Italians will understand you."

## 4. Stress: which syllable is loud? (0:45–1:20)

**PICTURE:** Page turn. Words appear as rows of paper blocks, one block per syllable. The stressed block pops up higher, with a small drum "boom".

> **n06 · NARRATOR:** "Every Italian word has one strong syllable. Most of the time, it's the one before last."

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "CA-sa. I-TA-lia. a-MI-co."
**SCREEN:** CA·sa · i·TA·lia · a·MI·co

> **n07 · NARRATOR:** "Sometimes it's one further back, like 'tavolo', table."

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "TA-vo-lo."
**SCREEN:** **TA**·vo·lo (table) 🪑

> **n08 · NARRATOR:** "And when the stress falls on the very last vowel, Italian writes an accent on it. Città. Caffè. Perché. Più."

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "Città. Caffè. Perché. Più."
**SCREEN:** città (city) · caffè (coffee) · perché (why / because) · più (more): **accent = stress here!**

> **n09 · NARRATOR:** "Little bonus: 'è' with an accent means 'is'. 'E' without one means 'and'."

**SCREEN:** **è** = is · **e** = and

## 5. Double letters (1:20–1:55)

**PICTURE:** Two paper cards side by side on a mini stage: **papa** and **pappa**. A pop-up bishop's hat (mitre) and a pop-up baby food bowl.

> **n10 · NARRATOR:** "Now the doubles. In Italian, a double consonant is held longer, with a tiny pause. And it changes the meaning."

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "Papa. Pappa."
**SCREEN:** *papa* [PAH-pah] = Pope (shown as a bishop's hat icon) · *pappa* [PAHP-pah] = baby food 🥣

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "Nono. Nonno."
**SCREEN:** *nono* [NOH-noh] = ninth 9️⃣ · *nonno* [NOHN-noh] = grandpa 👴

> **n11 · NARRATOR:** "Nono is 'ninth'. Nonno, with two Ns, is 'grandpa'. That's Orso's mistake!"

**PICTURE:** Orso facepalms; a paper "9th" ribbon floats away.

> **n12 · NARRATOR:** "Trick: say 'black cat' slowly. Feel that little stop between the two Ks? That's an Italian double. Gatto. Mamma."

**IT (TTS, Giulia):** "Gatto. Mamma."
**SCREEN:** *gatto* [GAHT-toh] cat 🐱 · *mamma* [MAHM-mah] mom
